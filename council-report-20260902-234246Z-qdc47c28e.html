Council Verdict

Where the council agrees

  • Adopt a stage-gated hybrid, not the full platform and not a permanently bare detector dashboard. Red Team, First Principles, Outsider and Executor all require an always-working baseline.
  • Establish the baseline by Day 2 and freeze expansion after Day 3. Red Team, First Principles, Outsider and the Defender support explicit cut points.
  • Retain secure UUID ingestion, compact track histories, deterministic policy, evidence clips and timestamps, decision traces, manifests and event-level tests. Red Team, First Principles, Expansionist and Executor converge here.
  • Keep targeted verification only when it produces a measured event-quality gain and place it behind a removable boundary. All five advisors converge on selective rather than universal two-pass processing.
  • Reduce semantic scope to one polished hero behavior plus one or two simpler behaviors. Red Team, Expansionist, Outsider and Executor prefer depth and proof over unreliable breadth.
  • Use one deterministic process, simple SQLite records and derived temporal features rather than a generalized graph or distributed verifier. Expansionist, Outsider, Executor and Defender support this.
  • Keep replay as a transparent development and failure fallback, never as an unlabeled substitute for live processing.

Where the council clashes

The main clash is between a broad, polished YOLO-plus-rules demo and a narrower proof-carrying hybrid. The simple-demo side correctly warns that histories, verification, manifests and evaluation can still become an integration project. The hybrid remains more persuasive because its additions are removable and the baseline survives, but only if the implementation stays monolithic and its evidence is visible.

Replay is also contested. It improves determinism and resilience, but can damage trust if it masks weak live performance. It is acceptable only when explicitly labeled and paired with target-machine live measurements.

Blind spots

The architecture still lacks predeclared gates for ID switches, track fragmentation, event precision/recall, p95 latency, repeatability and maximum processing time. It also lacks confirmed licensed footage, ground-truth ownership, target-hardware benchmarks, rubric evidence and a concrete audit-day protocol. Day 6 must include a clean-room fresh-checkout run, dependency and model-license review, privacy and upload tests, regression, manifest verification and degraded-mode rehearsal.

Recommendation

Choose a stage-gated hybrid centered on one proof-carrying hero behavior plus one or two simpler behaviors. Build a monolithic FastAPI/Python/SQLite pipeline with a thin Next.js evidence-review UI; cut the generalized Temporal Scene Graph, assistant, generated client, privacy blur, broad taxonomy and generalized rule framework from the critical path. Admit targeted verification only after it improves event quality within the latency budget.

One thing to do first

Within 48 hours, run a licensed representative clip on the target hardware and record predeclared tracking, event-quality, latency and upload → event → evidence acceptance results.

Advisor Responses
Red Team high
Premortem: the full system fails because tracking fragments, calibration varies, verification lags and thresholds remain untuned. Root cause is committing to the full architecture without a mandatory baseline. Cut scope if upload → event → evidence is not deterministic by Day 2. Use derived temporal features, not a general graph; keep simple SQLite and only measured two-pass verification.
First Principles high
Allocate by failure containment. Establish a baseline by Day 2, add verification behind removable interfaces and freeze scope after Day 3. The irreducible product is repeatable ingestion, event detection, time-linked explanation and reproducibility. Selective verification, traces, policies and manifests are the highest-leverage additions.
Expansionist high
The dominant outside option is a proof-carrying hero behavior paired with a replay-first temporal lab. One deeply evaluated temporal behavior preserves differentiation while ordinary SQLite records avoid a generalized graph. Make the assistant optional and center visible evidence.
Outsider high
Using experimental flight-test logic, freeze a Day-2 taxi-test baseline, then admit histories, verification, manifests and evaluation one gate at a time. General abstractions are mostly invisible to judges. Preserve abort modes and reduce to two behaviors with three curated scenarios.
Executor high
Orient for only half a day, then decide. The hero-slice hybrid ranks above both full and basic options. Unknown hardware throughput, licensed footage and ground-truth criteria block a final scope. Keep validation, histories, verification, risk, evidence/traces and tests; simplify or cut everything else.
